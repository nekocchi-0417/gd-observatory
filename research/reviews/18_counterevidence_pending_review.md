# Counter-evidence proposals: to be verified

Status: **To be verified. Removed from the public Evidence page on 13 October 2026.** These are archived editorial proposals, not validated tests of gradual disempowerment. This note is not included in the website build.

## Where the section came from

The internal [red-team review](13_redteam.md), section 5, proposed a box describing observations that might count against the site's framing. It offered examples, including a closing young-worker employment gap, stable unaided ability, and a low AI share of music streams. That review explicitly says it fetched nothing from the web.

The later [UX specification](17_ux_spec.md), Evidence-page section, moved the five-item list into an expandable footer. The list appears in the initial repository commit (`a5005c3`) and was still present before removal (`6b81fbd`). Its public wording provided no citations or derivation for the proposed thresholds.

The public introduction said: "The theory can fail. Each of these would count against it." That wording was stronger than the documented basis. Each proposal needs a defined claim, population, timeframe, and justification for how the observation would bear on that claim. A change in one indicator should not be presented as a validated test of the entire theory without that argument.

## Archived wording and open questions

The text in the middle column is preserved from the withdrawn section; it is not endorsed or newly verified here.

| Proposed sign | Withdrawn wording | What needs review |
|---|---|---|
| Young workers catch up | Jobs for 22 to 25 year olds in exposed fields rejoin their peers' path. | Define the comparison group, baseline, timeframe, and what would count as catching up. Specify which claim about AI and employment this would test. |
| Workers' share recovers | Its four-quarter average climbs back above the 2025 level. | Justify the 2025 baseline and four-quarter window. Explain why crossing that threshold would bear on the proposed AI mechanism. No source-linked justification was supplied in the section. |
| Skills hold up | Repeated tests find unaided ability stable among heavy AI users, as one Japanese clinic has. | Verify the clinic study and its relevance to the broader claim. Define the skills, users, follow-up period, and comparison needed to interpret stable scores. |
| Officials overrule the machine | Published override rates show officials changing a substantial share of AI advice. | Define "substantial" and explain what override rates would establish about human control. Specify how decision quality and the ability to intervene would be assessed. |
| Attention stays with people | AI-made work stays a small share of what people read and hear. | Define "small", the media and populations covered, and the time window. Explain the proposed connection between attention share and human influence. |

Before considering republication, review the primary sources and the proposed interpretation separately. Any future version should distinguish observations, proposed tests of particular mechanisms, and conclusions about the broader theory. Keep these proposals out of the public Evidence page until the author has reviewed that basis.
