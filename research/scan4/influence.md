## 3. Key studies

- **Lin et al. 2025 (*Nature*).** In the US, Canadian and Polish elections, a conversation with an AI shifted candidate preference more than typical video ads. AIs arguing for right-wing candidates made more inaccurate claims.
- **CETaS 2024 (Turing Institute).** Found no evidence that AI disinformation measurably changed any 2024 election result it tracked (US, UK, EU, Taiwan, India). It says data on voter behaviour are lacking.
- **Gilens & Page 2014.** Across 1,779 policy issues, economic elites and business groups had substantial independent influence on US policy. Average citizens had little or none, long before AI.
- **Korinek & Lockwood 2026 (NBER).** A theory paper: AI "may gradually erode the two main tax bases", labour first and then consumption. Nothing has been measured yet.
- **DOGE "AI Deregulation Decision Tool" (Washington Post, Jul 2025).** Leaked slides describe a mostly automated tool. It made "decisions on 1,083 regulatory sections" at HUD and was meant to flag about 100,000 rules for cutting. HUD says no decisions were completed.
- **Measurement frameworks.** Kulveit et al. 2025 (s6.2); Chooi, Lee & Li 2026 (perception surveys, capital flows); Moon & Boudreaux, RAND 2026 (the smallest group able to settle a decision, and its make-up). All are proposals; none has been fielded.

## 4. Sceptic's case

- **Voters and legislatures still bite.** The Senate voted 99–1 to remove a 10-year ban on state AI laws (2025). Thirty-eight states passed about 100 AI measures in 2025.
- **Local say is working.** Local opposition blocked or delayed about $130bn of data-centre projects in Q1 2026, about as much as in the whole of 2025.
- **No measured election effect.** CETaS found no 2024 result that AI disinformation measurably altered.
- **Lost say predates AI.** Trust in Washington fell from 77% (1964) to about 25% by 1980. V-Dem's 2026 decline is political and does not mention AI.
- **The state needs people more, not less.** The income and payroll tax share rose to 86% in FY2026.
- **Perception is not influence, and it is flat.** 59% → 57%.
- **Money is not proven to win.** The AI super PAC's top target lost his primary. That is one race, and nobody has tested whether the spending caused it.

**Pointing the other way:** AI lobbying share up from about 11% to 26%; large AI PAC money; a Dec 2025 executive order against state AI laws despite majority opposition in polls (via an advocacy compilation).

## 6. Proposed ratings

- **Measurement level: 0 → 1 ("One-offs or paperwork").** Flagged as an editorial call.
  - Why: there are now AI-specific one-offs (Pew asked twice; the UK survey once) and paperwork (lobbying disclosures, FEC filings). None measures influence itself.
  - Keep 0 if "direct" must mean "do outcomes follow people's preferences?"
  - Up to 2 if Pew asks a third time or a panel asks the same people about their say.
- **What the data show: keep "?".**
  - Why: the series that are moving (AI lobbying share, PAC money) measure efforts to gain influence, not results. The one AI-specific control item is flat, and the tax share moved against the theory.
  - What would change it: control or say items moving clearly over three or more readings, or a measured gap between what people want on AI and what is enacted.
