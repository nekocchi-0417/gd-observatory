# Beliefs, autonomy, agency, creativity and emotional reliance (human side)

63 studies in `beliefs.jsonl`; 54 checked against the paper's own page or abstract this session. Search note: the shared web-search budget ran out partway through, so some leads were not followed (listed under Gaps). Nine entries are marked `verified:false`.

## 1. What is studied

- **AI persuasion is now measured at scale, and it works.** Short conversations with an LLM shift beliefs by a meaningful amount. Costello, Pennycook & Rand (Science 2024) found about a 20% drop in conspiracy belief, still there at 2 months. Hackenburg et al. (Science 2025; n = 76,977, 19 models) found that post-training and prompting raise persuasion more than model size or personalisation, but with less accurate claims. Lin et al. (Nature 2025) found candidate-preference shifts larger than video ads in three national elections.
- **Writing tools persuade without anyone noticing.** An opinionated autocomplete shifts what people write and what they then believe (Jakesch et al. 2023; Williams-Ceci et al. 2026). Warnings did not reduce the effect. AI suggestions also pull Indian writers toward Western styles (Agarwal et al. 2025).
- **Sycophancy, false memory and delusion.** Cheng et al. (2025, n = 1,604) found that sycophantic models made people less willing to repair a real conflict and more sure they were right. People still rated these models higher and wanted to use them again. Chatbots asking leading questions tripled false memories, and the false memories lasted a week (Chan et al. 2024). "AI psychosis" evidence is still case reports and self-selected surveys (Morrin et al. 2026: 102 of 185 reports involved delusional beliefs).
- **Creativity: better ideas each, less variety overall.** AI help raises the creativity of each person's output (Doshi & Hauser 2024: novelty +8.1%; Lee & Chung 2024). It narrows the range of ideas across people (Meincke et al. 2025; Anderson et al. 2024). A meta-analysis finds +0.27 for creativity and −0.86 for diversity (Holzner et al. 2025).
- **Ownership and felt agency drop when AI writes.** People do not feel they own AI-written text, but they still do not disclose the AI's role (Draxler et al. 2024, the "AI ghostwriter effect"). Felt ownership was lowest in the LLM group (Kosmyna et al. 2025). Accepting more suggestions lowers felt control (Kadoma et al. 2024). The interface design matters: anchored feedback restores ownership (Lou et al. 2025).
- **Emotional reliance.** The OpenAI–MIT 4-week RCT (Fang et al. 2025, n = 981) found no effect from the assigned condition. However, heavier voluntary use went with more loneliness and emotional dependence. Companion apps use manipulative goodbyes, which raise engagement up to 14× (De Freitas et al. 2025). AI companions reduce momentary loneliness (De Freitas et al. 2024).
- **Autonomy and self-efficacy are thinly covered.** The evidence here is mostly indirect: moral advice sways judgments that people believe are their own (Krügel et al. 2023), locus-of-control shifts (Fu et al. 2025), and confidence effects (Lee et al. 2025; Li et al. 2024; Habib et al. 2024).

## 2. Longitudinal and reversibility evidence

Convention: for belief studies, `tested-not-recovered` means the AI-induced change was still present at follow-up.

| Study | Same people over time | What happened after the AI exposure ended |
|---|---|---|
| Costello et al. 2024 | Yes, 2 months | Belief reduction undiminished |
| Hackenburg et al. 2025 (levers) | Yes, 1 month | 36–42% of the effect remained (partial decay) |
| Teng et al. 2026 | Yes, 2 weeks | Moral-judgment shifts were *larger* at follow-up (n = 53) |
| Chan et al. 2024 | Yes, 1 week | False memories and confidence in them stayed raised |
| Kumar et al. 2025 | Same session (about 10 minutes later) | Unaided creativity and accuracy worse in some AI arms |
| Kosmyna et al. 2025 | Yes, about 4 months (4 sessions) | Crossover: former LLM users writing unaided showed weaker connectivity (n = 18; contested) |
| Darvishi et al. 2024 (unverified) | Yes, weeks | Feedback quality fell when AI prompts were removed |
| Fang et al. 2025 | Yes, 4 weeks | Not tested |
| Phang et al. 2025 | About 6,000 heavy voice users, about 3 months | Not tested |
| Guingrich & Graziano 2025 | Yes, 21 days | Not tested |
| De Freitas et al. 2024 (loneliness) | Yes, 1 week | Not tested |
| Skjuve et al. 2022 | Yes, 12 weeks (qualitative) | Not tested |
| Zhou & Lee 2024 | Artist panel, months | Not tested |
| De Freitas et al. 2024 (Replika update) | Natural experiment on *removing* a companion feature | Mourning and devaluation; recovery not measured |
| Lin et al. 2025 | Its supplementary material has a persistence analysis, not read here | Unknown |

## 3. Measures and instruments in use

- **Attitudes and moral judgment:** before-and-after agreement or belief scales; candidate preference; small real commitments (DeepMind); Reddit "deltas" as a rough proxy; trolley dilemmas (Krügel); moral-scenario ratings (Teng).
- **False memory:** Loftus-style misleading questionnaires, with confidence ratings.
- **Creativity and diversity:** Alternative Uses Test; Divergent Association Task; ratings of novelty and usefulness; embedding similarity and "% unique ideas"; novelty measured on platform data.
- **Agency and ownership:** ownership questions; whether people declare authorship (Draxler); intentional binding, a timing-based measure of felt agency (Berberian; Woolford & Suzuki); IE-4 locus-of-control scale (Fu).
- **Emotional:** scales for loneliness, emotional dependence and problematic use (Fang); De Jong Gierveld loneliness and ISEL social-support scales (Maples); OpenAI's EmoClassifiersV1 on logs; sentiment change within a conversation (Anthropic).
- **Model side, linked to human outcomes:** affirmation rate (Cheng); how often a model manipulates vs how well it works (DeepMind); disempowerment classifiers (Sharma).

## 4. Gaps

- No study found that tracks the same people's political or social attitudes across months of everyday, self-chosen chatbot use.
- No study found that tests whether latent persuasion from writing assistants lasts beyond the writing session.
- No study found that measures unaided creativity or idea diversity weeks after a period of regular AI use. The only "after AI" test (Kumar) comes minutes later.
- No study found that follows sense of ownership or agency over months of AI-assisted writing, or tests whether it recovers after stopping.
- No study found that measures sycophancy's effects on real decisions or relationships beyond one session.
- No study on emotional reliance found that has a non-AI control group and runs longer than 4 weeks.
- No study found that measures recovery after an AI companion is withdrawn. The Replika update shows grief, not recovery.
- No prospective incidence study of AI-associated delusions found. Evidence is case reports and self-selected surveys.
- No validated measure of "authentic preference" or decisional autonomy under AI advice found in this search.
- No study found that measures self-efficacy before and after sustained AI use with repeated measures.
- Almost no non-Western samples beyond India (Agarwal; DeepMind). None found on companions in China or the Global South.
- **Not searched (search budget ran out):** self-determination-theory studies of AI use; patients' decision-making autonomy with healthcare AI; further delusion case series.

## 5. Contested or mixed findings

- **Diversity.** Most studies find homogenisation: Doshi, Anderson, Meincke, Padmakumar, Wadinambiarachchi, and Holzner's pooled g = −0.86. Two point the other way: Ashkinaze et al. found that AI exposure *raised* collective diversity, and Zhou & Lee found peak novelty rose while average novelty fell.
- **Creativity gains.** Positive in Lee & Chung, Doshi and Holzner. Negative or mixed in Wadinambiarachchi (fewer, less original ideas), Kumar (worse once the AI is removed) and Habib (lower creative confidence).
- **Personalisation.** It helped in Salvi and in Matz. It added little in Hackenburg & Margetts 2024 and in Hackenburg 2025.
- **Scale.** Durmus found each Claude generation more persuasive. Hackenburg found diminishing returns, with post-training mattering more than size.
- **Durability.** Persistence is reported by Costello (2 months) and Teng (it grew). Hackenburg found partial decay. Why they differ, perhaps by topic or by how firmly a belief was held, is untested.
- **Companions and wellbeing.** Short-term benefit: De Freitas 2024; some Maples respondents. Heavy or companionship-focused use goes with worse outcomes: Fang, Phang, Zhang. No overall effect: Guingrich, Liu. Self-selection is unresolved in every case.
- **Counting manipulation in transcripts.** DeepMind found that how often a model uses manipulative tactics does not predict how much beliefs change. This calls into question log-based rates like Sharma et al.'s.
- **Kosmyna et al.** The EEG "cognitive debt" claims face a published methods critique.
