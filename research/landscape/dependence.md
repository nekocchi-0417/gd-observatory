# Dependence, habitual reliance, longitudinal tracking and reversibility

## 1. What is studied

- **Dependence scales: many, all one-off.** At least eight since 2024 (Peru, Korea, China ×3, US/Singapore, Germany, Colombia), mostly adapted from social-media addiction scales. None has a validated cut-off. The most thoroughly tested is GAIDS (Goh et al. 2025: 6 studies, 2 countries).
- **Student panels: short and self-report.** About five, mostly Chinese, lasting 4 weeks to 10 months. Dependence and answer-seeking use predict lower later self-efficacy (AERA 2026 PhD panel; Abbas 2024). Learning-focused use predicts higher self-efficacy (Zhao 2026). In adolescents, poor mental health predicted later dependence, not the reverse (Huang 2024).
- **Withdrawal in trials (strongest evidence).** Bastani 2025: −17% on a closed-book exam after plain-chatbot practice, no harm with a tutor version. Shen & Tamkin 2026: 50% vs 67% on a no-AI quiz. Darvishi 2024: students had relied on AI prompts rather than learned. All tests come minutes to weeks after use.
- **Real-world withdrawal.** Support agents kept their gains during AI outages (Brynjolfsson et al. 2025). Italy's 2023 ChatGPT ban cut developer output by about half for two days, then output recovered as VPN and Tor use rose (Kreitmeir & Raschky 2023). A four-day withdrawal diary study found disruption and renewed professional values (Oh et al. 2026).
- **Expert skill over years:** Poland shows a drop in unaided detection within months (Budzyń 2025); Japan shows none over 3 years (Okumura 2025).
- **Logs track conversations, not people.** Directive use on Claude rose from 27% to 39% in eight months. Auto-approve went from about 20% to over 40% of sessions with experience. Fact-checking appears in 8.7% of conversations. Disempowerment potential rose over 2025 (Sharma et al. 2026).
- **Emotional dependence:** 4-week OpenAI–MIT RCT found no condition effects, but heavier self-chosen use went with worse outcomes. About 0.15% of weekly ChatGPT users show heightened attachment. Replika users grieved a removed feature (De Freitas et al. 2024).

## 3. Measures and instruments in use

- **Dependence scales:** DIA (5 items), Zhang 2024 (6 items), AI Chatbot Dependence Scale (8), CAIDS-20, Chinese undergraduate AIDS (22), GAIDS, LLM-D12, Colombian 32-item scale.
- **Unaided tests:** closed-book exams, no-AI quizzes, creativity tasks, adenoma detection without AI, ALEKS proctored retention items, GPS navigation tasks, simulator hand-flying.
- **Logs:** directive vs learning shares (Economic Index), auto-approve and interrupt rates, AI Fluency behaviours, disempowerment and affective-cue classifiers.
- **Panels:** WorkAI, AlgoSoc/LISS, the Finnish adult panel, NEPS (planned), Pew AI-control items (separate samples each time).
- **Frameworks:** Chooi, Lee & Li 2026 (exposure / erosion / lock-in); Kulveit et al. 2025. No RAND metric set was found in this search.

## 4. Gaps

- No study found that re-tests the same people's unaided skill at regular intervals for more than a few months while recording their generative-AI use; the 3-year evidence is from colonoscopy AI and GPS, not LLMs.
- No study found that measures take-back time: how long performance takes to recover after AI is removed. Every withdrawal test is a single check minutes to weeks later.
- No dependence scale found with a validated cut-off or a representative sample. Only two panels re-measure dependence: Huang 2024 (adolescents in 2022, before ChatGPT) and the AERA 2026 PhD panel.
- No study found linking a dependence score to measured (not self-rated) unaided ability in the same people.
- No public per-person trajectory of reliance (delegation, checking, overrides) from any AI provider. All published log trends are per conversation.
- No panel found outside Finland and the Netherlands that follows adults' AI use and perceived say over years; none links it to tested skills.
- No study found of judgement (deference vs override on known-correct and incorrect AI advice) measured repeatedly in the same people.
- No study of lock-in (switching costs, or loss of non-AI fallback processes in organisations) was found in this search.

## 5. Contested or mixed findings

- **Expert deskilling:** Budzyń (decline) vs Okumura (none over 3 years); outage data show gains persisting (Brynjolfsson).
- **Causal direction:** Huang finds mental health leads to dependence; Wang 2026 and the AERA panel model dependence as the cause of harm.
- **Use type vs use:** answer-seeking use is linked to harm (Bastani base arm; Abbas); guided or learning-focused use is not (Bastani tutor arm; Zhao). But Kumar's coach-style arm hurt later convergent thinking.
- **Withdrawal by experience:** the Italian ban raised novices' output and lowered experts' routine-task productivity (Kreitmeir & Raschky 2024).
- **Rising delegation is ambiguous:** it could be learned trust or dependence. Experienced Claude Code users also interrupt more.
- **Kosmyna's "cognitive debt"** rests on 18 people in session 4, and its analysis has been publicly criticised.
