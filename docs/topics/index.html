Skills and judgement

How does using AI affect learning and judgement?

AI can help people perform a task, while its effects on learning vary with the task and tool design. The studies selected here offer a less clear picture of how independent judgement changes with prolonged use.

02 Selected studies

Proposed shortlist

Chosen for direct tests of learning or performance, clear comparisons, and findings that help explain when AI assistance helps or hinders.

Why these studies?

The experiments test what people can do after AI is removed. The meta-analysis puts assisted performance in context. Supporting studies add positive learning findings, workplace evidence and an observational comparison.

This is a non-exhaustive, AI-assisted selection. No completed human review is recorded for this prototype. Publication status, study limits and the reason for selection accompany each source.

03 Measurement gaps

Questions to investigate

Questions the selected studies leave open. Each gap is a provisional interpretation to test against the wider literature.

Do learning effects last?

Skills retained after months of routine AI use.

Basis in the evidenceBastani and Shen test learning soon after assistance. Lira also tests gains the next day. These follow-ups leave longer-term retention and recovery unresolved.

Proposed measurement: Repeated unaided tests before, during and after months of AI use, with a comparison group.

What else needs checking?

The longer-duration records in the library need review before calling this a field-wide gap. Study duration alone does not establish that the same people were tested repeatedly or after AI was withdrawn.

Does judgement of AI advice improve?

Accepting useful advice and recognising errors.

Basis in the evidenceVaccaro compares final task performance. METR compares perceived and actual productivity. Neither finding establishes how a person's ability to evaluate AI advice changes with continued use.

Proposed measurement: Repeated decisions with correct and misleading AI advice, tracking error detection, appropriate reliance and confidence.

What else needs checking?

The trust and reliance literature may already test parts of this question. Check repeated behavioural measures separately from one-time surveys of trust.

Can people maintain oversight at work?

Having both the skill and authority to challenge AI.

Basis in the evidenceThe featured studies examine classroom learning, a coding exercise and combined task performance. They do not jointly track professional skill and the authority to review or override AI decisions.

Proposed measurement: Follow one profession, pairing unaided work samples with records of human review, overrides and training.

What else needs checking?

Review the oversight-related records and relevant organisational research before choosing a setting. Missing links in this shortlist do not establish their absence across the field.
